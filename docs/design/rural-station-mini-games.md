# Mini-games at the station

**Design proposal · For review**

The [station setting](rural-station-setting.md) gives the existing games reasons to happen in the same village. The siblings and Pitsi move between familiar places while Dad works the station counter. The postman keeps his role and personality. Each game below has a standalone round. A chapter can borrow a short activity when it answers a question in the case, as the receipt does in the [parcel opening](rural-station-opening-case.md).

| Existing game | Station proposal | Player's main activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| צובעים ארגזים | **ארגזים למחסן** | Copy useful labels onto storage boxes and put the boxes where someone can find them. |
| הדואר המבולבל | **גלויות לדוור** | Listen to the returning postman and reconstruct words on public village postcards. |
| עקבות בחשיכה · hidden | **בעקבות פיצי** | Compare a trace with the public village map and type the matching waypoint. |
| פיצי במרדף · hidden | **שליחות עם פיצי** | Deliver a note along a route of your choice, with an optional personal pace challenge. |
| חזרה באסם | **חזרה בכיכר** | Listen to English songs with the village band and direct what the performers do. |
| הטרמפ של נוגה | **איפה נוגה?** | Use witness statements and objects to work out why a singer moved and where to find her. |

## Storage boxes · ארגזים למחסן

The anniversary organizer needs exhibit supplies sorted on the station's storage shelves. Dad wheels a few boxes to the public counter. One sibling reads the packing card while the other labels the boxes. After the player presses Enter, the label appears on the box and Dad wheels it into the matching shelf bay. Pitsi inspects a box marked **כובעים** and waits expectantly until a sibling reminds him he already has one at home.

The source stays visible, with the current Hebrew key and finger guidance available as in the [existing crate game](../crate-typing.he.md). Try a short batch with one label per box, such as **דגלים**, **חוטים**, **תצלומים**. Finish by finding one requested box on the completed shelf and sending it to the square. Each level can add one demand: short labels, longer labels, then a short two-word label where the modifier matters to the organizer. Keep the batch size and pace fixed during that comparison. Labels count as copying for typing practice. Free help can attach the label and keep the round moving.

**Why play another batch?** The boxes become a shelf the player can actually use. In a paper comparison, keep the same words and reward and swap a row of finished boxes for a shelf with a final retrieval. Ask which version made the labels feel worth writing.

## Postman's postcards · גלויות לדוור

The same postman collects village mail by the station hall. For the anniversary he is helping assemble a rack of public postcards by local contributors. A splash erased one word per caption. He has the contributor's recorded line, so the player can listen, type the word and submit it. Each restored word fills a visible gap, and the completed card goes onto the rack. The postman sees a sketch of an enormous drum and says he hopes nobody expects him to fit it through a letterbox.

Use the existing spelling loop, context sentences, replay and Hebrew keyboard support. For example, hear **ליד התחנה מחכה תזמורת** and enter **תזמורת** into **ליד התחנה מחכה ___**. A replay does not expose the spelling. Showing a letter, showing the whole word, and a short recall after a reveal carry their existing aid distinctions. Speech off or a missing clip offers the written word to copy. The player can stop a clip, correct and resubmit, or skip and see the completed card. Start with everyday postcard words from the current recording bank when they fit. A later round can add more confusable spellings with routine length and pace held steady.

**Why restore a card?** A word makes the picture and message make sense. Compare the same spoken prompts with and without mounting completed illustrated cards. Ask which card the player wants to reread and whether the postman made the round feel like a visit.

## Pitsi's trail · בעקבות פיצי

The square is missing a roll of ribbon for the exhibit. At a fork in the public footpath, Pitsi pauses by a loose ribbon thread snagged on a marker. A close-up shows blue paint on the snag. The player compares two labeled map photos, a blue bench and an unpainted stone gate, then types **ספסל** or **שער**. At the bench, a new trace narrows the next choice. At the end the café owner reveals where she set the roll aside, and Pitsi gets all the credit for noticing the trail.

Keep deliberate Hebrew typing with Enter and an optional unhighlighted keyboard, as in the old Tracks mode. The location words are visible throughout. The new challenge is choosing the word from evidence; the player may also select the waypoint first and then copy its name. A plausible wrong stop lets the siblings inspect it and return with their draft intact. A keyboard error gets typing help, while a wrong stop gets a clue comparison. Assistance can highlight the matching landmark and finish the step. First use two clearly different landmarks, then keep the same word length and number of choices while making the clue require a feature comparison, such as the bench by the orchard wall.

**Why follow the next clue?** Each stop should change the player's idea of where the ribbon went. With identical words and route, compare a route that names the next stop with one that asks the player to identify it from a clue. Watch whether the child proposes another lead independently.

## Courier with Pitsi · שליחות עם פיצי

Dad has a note for the band in the square. The siblings choose the direct orchard path or the café lane before departure. Each short sign word is a cue to move to the next stop, with the existing Chase behavior of advancing automatically on a complete correct entry. The route and next cue remain visible. At the café, Pitsi gives the sandwich board a deep bow, convinced it is waiting for a performance. The selected route determines which brief event the player sees.

Start with two routes that use equally many words of similar length. A later round can add one junction where the player chooses which sign to type next. The letter guide and an assisted finish are free. Relaxed play is the default, and the note arrives at any pace. The optional timed replay compares only the same route, word sequence and support conditions with the player's own previous run. It has neither a deadline nor a faster route required for a better story outcome. Pause freezes movement and timing, and reduced motion shows the next stop and its reaction in a settled pose.

**Why replay?** The child can see what is down the other lane or try a familiar route more fluently. Compare a route chosen by the player with that same route assigned in the briefing. Keep words and pace identical and ask whether choosing made the delivery feel like theirs.

## Village band rehearsal · חזרה בכיכר

The village band is rehearsing for a small show in the square. The siblings lead the sound check and direct the scene. Dad turns up with a suitcase that he insists is a perfectly respectable drum. Pitsi sits wherever the next prop is meant to go. The miniature stage gives the player instant feedback when a change to an English line changes the action.

Keep the [current rehearsal flow](../english-grade-7/song-mini-game.he.md): pick a song, listen to a short excerpt, identify a meaning or the sequence of events, direct a variation, and pick moments for an encore. For the first station round, propose one original song about the sound check. The player hears an original line such as `Put the drum beside the suitcase.` and matches the setup before swapping `beside` for `behind` and watching the cast move the drum. The next song could swap who carries what while keeping the same level of reading support. In the encore, Dad discovers that the suitcase sounds best when nobody is sitting on it.

Keep the replay controls and accessible bilingual booklet. The interface is Hebrew/RTL and English prompts are LTR. Assistance is tracked for the relevant English task. Listening, reading, grammar and vocabulary stay separate from the Hebrew typing and spelling reports. Station lyrics, translation and audio are new authored content for the pilot. The current six-song library is a reference for format and implementation. Any track brought forward needs suitable lyrics, audio rights and a stage scene that fits.

**Why direct another line?** The player gets to make a joke happen and can see exactly which word caused it. Compare the same question with a still result versus a short performed result. Ask what `behind` changed and which version the child wants in the encore.

## Finding Noga · איפה נוגה?

Propose Noga as a visiting young singer who came by train for the anniversary rehearsal. Her chair in the square is empty, her blue scarf is still on the café bench, and she took her music folder. The band wants to bring her the correct song sheets. Dad confirms she arrived, and the siblings and Pitsi investigate three connected public spots: the square, the café and a covered shelter beside the station forecourt. The case is separate from the chapter about parcel 27.

**What happened.** A shower interrupted the square's sound check. Noga carried her folder to the covered shelter to keep rehearsing. The café owner saw someone with the blue scarf and assumed it was Noga. The player can inspect the scarf on the bench and learn that the passerby had a café apron. The stage organizer remembers Noga leaving with her star-stickered music folder when the rain began. An awning notice gives the alternative sound-check spot, and from the sheltered path the player can see Noga's star-stickered folder propped on a music stand. Pitsi tries to follow a toast crumb past the café, an optional aside.

| Deduction | Evidence that supports it |
| --- | --- |
| **למה נוגה עזבה את הכיכר?** | The rain-marked stage cue card, the organizer's account of the interrupted sound check, and the notice telling performers to move under cover during rain. |
| **איפה נוגה עכשיו?** | The same notice names the forecourt shelter, the organizer identifies the star on Noga's folder, and the player observes the matching folder on the sheltered stand. |

Reuse the [detective adventure](../noga-adventure-redesign.he.md) structure: explore in any order, bookmark lines with their sources, explicitly take permitted objects, and connect evidence on two revisable boards by drag or keyboard/click. The same collected notice can support both deductions. A photograph or notebook observation can stand in for a portable object where it encodes the same fact. The player may reach the shelter early and check the stand from outside; the proof prompts are about explaining how Noga got there, and the closing scene brings her the song sheets. Free graded hints and **נחבר יחד** preserve progression. No typing, timer or score is required for the investigation.

**Why investigate?** A clue that first looks decisive can be corrected by something the player personally sees. Ask what made them change their mind about the scarf and whether they can explain both conclusions before the recap. Compare the same case with the café lead or the square lead suggested first, with either place still open.

## What to test first after approval

1. Use storage and postcards to check that the station makes the current typing and spelling games more purposeful. Try a complete short round in each before expanding the vocabulary bank.
2. Use cards to test the two hidden-game replacements and Noga's evidence chain. Add the redesigned Tracks and Courier to the public menu only after their different choices are legible and children have a reason to replay.
3. Try one original song and one encore on the village stage. Use the current booklet, replay and pause behaviors as the baseline.

*Epistory* makes the keyboard part of exploring and interacting with a world. Here, typing a visible landmark selects the lead and typing a sign moves the party. This gives Hebrew key practice an immediate result at a child-controlled pace. [Fishing Cactus's Epistory page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/398850/Epistory__Typing_Chronicles/)

*Duck Detective: The Secret Salami* builds comic deduction around inspecting evidence and completing an explanation. Noga's case adapts that rhythm to two small, answerable questions and keeps its evidence accessible through the existing notebook and help. [Happy Broccoli Games](https://www.happybroccoligames.com/)

These are written proposals. For a future build, reuse the learning validators and saved-run behavior where the tasks still match. Keep guidance free, use local saves, preserve keyboard access and verify Hebrew input, correction, help and resume across the supported desktop sizes. Guidance, pace and round length should use the same bounded setup for adults and tests, with effective settings saved and copyable and any restart stated before applying. Human play is needed to answer the questions above.
