# The parcel for the old station

**Paper case · For review · Contains the solution**

This is a worked proposal for the first chapter of [The Station](rural-station-setting.md). The shared parcel-and-photograph hook comes from the supplied concept. The anniversary exhibit, parcel contents, evidence and resolution below are proposed additions. The case can be read or tried with paper cards during setting review.

## The first minute

The siblings are in the station hall waiting for Dad's shift to finish. Across the square, the village is putting together an exhibit for the station's anniversary. Dad places a parcel on the counter with the name **תחנת הגבעה** on its label. The sign outside says **תחנת הגבעות**. He recognizes **תחנת הגבעה** as the station's old name and suggests the sender used an old address. Before the player leaves the hall, the café owner arrives with prints for the exhibit and says she took them on Tuesday. The top print shows the old sign above this week's anniversary poster. The square is already visible as a place to go, and the next question is on the siblings' lips.

> **אבא** — הגבעה? ככה קראו לתחנה פעם. אולי העתיקו כתובת ישנה.
>
> **אחד האחים** — אז למה השלט הישן מופיע בתמונה מיום שלישי?
>
> **השני** — אולי יש עוד תחנה.
>
> **אבא** — גם בה האריח ליד הדלת עקום?

The player can compare the photo with the entrance or ask the organizer in the square why the parcel matters. Both leads stay available. The organizer is introduced when the children first enter the square and is named on the exhibit notice in the hall.

## What happened

On Tuesday, the organizer temporarily hung the retired sign for an anniversary photo before a small exhibit preview. The café owner photographed the trial with the dated preview poster in the frame. While the organizer was in the square getting the display ready, a volunteer carried a wrapped reproduction of the original village walking map from the station lobby. The café owner saw the bundle and assumed it had just come off the train. The organizer heard that account and asks the siblings to check which version of the map was present at the preview before she prints the caption.

The original map came in by train on Friday. It is in parcel **27** on Dad's counter. The lending family intentionally lettered its outer wrap with the old station name for the exhibit and added the organizer's current collection slip. The organizer and Dad can check those facts on the exterior tags. The siblings prove which version was shown on Tuesday before the Friday display opens. The immediate result is a corrected caption and the arrival of the original map in the square, where the exhibit can show it alongside the reproduction.

## A word task with a reason to exist

Before comparing the records, the player can make a receipt for the exhibit. The notebook asks for the confirmed object's short label, **מפת שבילים**, using the exterior slip as a source. On confirmation, the receipt visibly joins parcel **27** to its Friday record. This is supported copying and any future implementation would record it accordingly. It takes one meaningful entry. The child can correct the field, open the Hebrew keyboard guide or choose **נעתיק יחד** to get the same receipt and evidence. A separate optional independent spelling activity, if added later, would use a fresh word the player has not just seen.

## Reconstruction and payoff

The organizer has two draft captions to edit, **המפה המקורית הוצגה ביום שלישי** and **ביום שלישי הוצג העתק. המפה המקורית הגיעה ביום שישי**. The player picks the supported claim and links the signed preview checklist and parcel 27's Friday entry. A second short notebook question asks why the old name appears on the parcel and in the photo. If a clue is missing, the first hint points to its source, the next puts the relevant details side by side, and **נחבר יחד** shows the reasoning and lets the story continue. There is no timer on a lead or deduction.

Back in the square, the organizer replaces the caption and the siblings get to see the original map laid out. One notices a footpath marked with a name the other has only heard from an older neighbor. The opening's two questions have answers, and the path gives the longer story somewhere to go.

## Paper playtest

Use the photo prints, parcel label and tags, café owner's account, checklist, dated record, timetable and caption choices from this outline. Start with the parcel and the print. Hand over each source when the child chooses the corresponding lead. Let them assemble and revise the case aloud. Observe where help was needed and whether the child voluntarily chooses another lead after the first deduction.

- After the opening, ask what they want to find out and where they would look first. Did the old name or the photo make them curious?
- Before the recap, ask them to explain why the parcel could not be the bundle from the preview and to point to the detail that changed their mind.
- Ask what they remember about each sibling and whether they would follow the path lead or stay in the square. Look for a reason tied to the story, a person or the place.
- Compare opening with the photograph immediately versus after one short conversation. Keep the rest of the case and the text of the clues the same. Observe when the child first volunteers a theory or chooses a lead without prompting.
- In a separate comparison, keep the case and receipt identical and give the player a choice of copying the label or attaching the prewritten label. Ask whether entering it felt like doing something for the organizer. Check that both routes leave the child equally able to explain the evidence.
